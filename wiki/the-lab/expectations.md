# Expectations

## What is expected of you

The lab expects integrity, professionalism, and engagement.

### Integrity

Approach all tasks with the intent to do them as well as possible. If it isn't worth doing right, it isn't worth doing.

We all fail at times. You are expected to fail, so that you may learn and improve - failing is good, as long as you improve from it! Figuring out what's failing quickly will allow you to avoid wasting time on bad ideas or approaches. When you fail, acknowledge this outcome to yourself and, if necessary, to others who may be affected.

I (Zhiwen) and the lab will provide training on methods for doing high-quality rigorous science, and it is ultimately your responsibility to ensure that your data are high-quality and your methods are appropriate and rigorous. So don't take short-cuts on understanding the equipment, methods, and analyses that you use - ask for help when you need to know more details! No part of your experiments should feel like a "black box".

Scientific misconduct will never be tolerated, and it is grounds for immediate dismissal. This includes plagiarism, data falsification or fabrication, and misrepresentation of your work, your contributions, or your actions.

### Professionalism

Treat others with respect. Clean up after yourself. Do your work on time or, if it is not possible, inform those affected as soon as possible and work to find a new plan.

For animal work, we should all treat experimental animals with respect, follow the protocol closely, communicate about health issues, and log welfare-relevant information.

There is no specific lab-mandated method for note-taking, but you are expected to take thorough and detailed notes in a method of your choosing. Imagine that a lab mate or even someone outside the lab is reading these and trying to check or understand something you've done.

### Engagement

Learn from and collaborate positively with your lab mates. Be a part of our exciting scientific community! Show up. Learn about everyone's projects and expertise, and share yours. Communicate clearly about any problems that you have, with the work or with your labmates.

Dive deep on your own project. Take ownership. Plan to become the world expert in your topic - whether you are a postdoc, PhD student, or undergraduate. Take initiative to learn everything that's relevant to know, and to get connected with people who can help you do that.

## What you can expect from the lab

The lab intends to help you learn and grow as a scientist while contributing to the body of human knowledge. We aim to provide an environment in which you are comfortable and have the support you need to succeed - you should feel free from harassment and aggression of any kind.

We aim to train you in both specific skills (including both experimental and computational/statistical) and in general approaches to science (such as critical thinking and scientific communication).

As a lab we are committed to making science a more open and inclusive field. You are encouraged to spend a portion of your work hours on mentoring, outreach, and education.

You are always encouraged to talk to Zhiwen if any of these expectations aren't being met, or if you have other expectations that aren't described here.
